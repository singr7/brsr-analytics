BRSR Lens, explained end to end

A portal that reads every listed company's sustainability filing, turns it into comparable numbers, and keeps the original evidence attached to each one. This guide walks the product screen by screen, then opens the hood for the team that will run it.

Orientation

What this is

Every large listed Indian company must file a Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report with SEBI each year. The filings are public, structured as XBRL, and effectively unreadable at scale — hundreds of documents, thousands of tagged facts each, inconsistent units, and no comparison layer.

BRSR Lens ingests those filings from the official NSE source, maps the tagged facts into a governed set of definitions, converts them to common units, and publishes comparable metrics and scores. The distinguishing commitment is the one below.

The product promise in one line

Every published number can be clicked back to the exact fact, in the exact filing, with the exact checksum it came from — and anything that cannot be resolved honestly is withheld rather than guessed.

That promise is why the platform is built the way it is: pinned field versions, versioned scoring methods, per-issuer unit registries, cohort suppression, and three human sign-off gates. Those are not compliance decoration. They are the reason a sustainability lead or an analyst can cite a figure from this portal.

Three pillars, one platform

Pillar 1

Insights

Analytics across the filed corpus — sector scorecards, company deep-dives, peer benchmarking, materiality and assurance views, plus natural-language querying, all with click-through lineage.

Pillar 2

Filing Studio

A guided preparation workspace: a company uploads its own documents and the system drafts its BRSR — section questionnaire, AI-assisted field mapping with confidence and human review, validations, and SEBI-format exports.

Pillar 3

Engagement engine

First-party product analytics, lead scoring and conversion paths. High-intent behaviour — peer-gap views, gap reports, deep-dive requests — routes to the business development team with context.

Orientation

Who it serves, and what each person wants

Four audiences use this portal, and the interface deliberately does not fork by persona. Everyone sees the same journeys; depth is one action away rather than behind an onboarding wizard.

Primary

The sustainability lead

Knows BRSR. Wants to know where their company stands against peers, which disclosures are thin, and what the credible sector benchmark actually is. Enters at Explore, ends at Peer benchmarks or a gap report.

Primary

The reporting / finance team

Has to produce next year's filing. Wants the drafting burden reduced without losing control of what gets said. Lives in Filing Studio, cares about validations and exports.

Secondary

The analyst or investor

Wants comparable, citable environmental numbers and a stated methodology. Lives in Ask and the dataset/API surfaces on the Research tier.

Secondary

The business sponsor

Wants the one-slide version: how many companies, how trustworthy, what it lets us say that nobody else can. Served by the home page, the sector scorecards, and the demo walkthrough.

Seventeen sectors are represented, weighted the way the index is: Financial Services is 23 of 100 companies, and eleven sectors have four or fewer. That skew matters for every cross-sector comparison the product shows, and the suppression rules in publishing rules exist because of it.

Under the hood — where these counts live
  • companies — one row per issuer: official name, NSE symbol, sector, industry, CIN (replaces the temporary ISIN once the XBRL CorporateIdentityNumber fact is parsed), market-cap band.
  • filings — one row per company-FY: source URL, SHA-256 checksum, submission and revision dates, pipeline status, fact counts.
  • xbrl_facts — every reported concept with raw value, unit, context, period and dimensional members. Nothing is discarded at parse time even when no mapping exists.
  • extracted_fields → field_version_pins → metrics / scores — the publishing chain. A metric cannot exist without a pin; a database trigger enforces that the pin, the extracted field and the filing all agree.
  • Raw artifacts live in object storage under raw/{identifier}/{fy}/{filename} and are immutable.

All of it is one Postgres 16 database with pgvector. There is no separate search cluster and no data warehouse; that is a deliberate architecture decision recorded in 00_ARCHITECTURE.md.

Every result carries four things the user cannot dismiss: the cohort it describes, the takeaway in one sentence, the limitation that stops the number being over-read, and the sources. Loading, empty, suppressed, tier-locked and error states are all explicit — nothing silently renders as zero.

Follow-ups turn a chart into a conversation: "normalise this by turnover", "keep comparisons within a sector". Each follow-up is a real query merged onto the current one, not a canned answer.

Under the hood — the guided question registry

Questions are versioned code, not database content: frontend/src/content/guided-questions.ts, registry version 1.0.0. Adding a question means adding an entry with a stable ID and shipping it — reviewable in a pull request, traceable in analytics.

  • State lives in the URL: ?question=<id>&q=<encoded DSL>, so any view is shareable and reproducible.
  • Eight questions are public; five environment questions require a paid tier and show a labelled lock rather than disappearing.
  • Analytics events carry the stable question ID only. Raw question text, company names and query text are prohibited in event payloads.

The job: an expert asks anything, in English, and gets an answer they can defend.

Ask is the full governed-query workspace. A natural-language question goes to /api/nlq, which translates it into the semantic DSL, validates it against the measure catalog, applies tier and cohort policy, compiles it to SQL, executes it, and returns the result with its lineage. The model chooses a query; it never invents a number.

Under the hood — why the LLM cannot fabricate a figure

The language model's only output is a semantic query object. That object then passes through the same path as a hand-built query:

  • Catalog validation — unknown measures or dimensions are rejected outright.
  • Tier policy — paid measures return a lock state, not data.
  • Cohort suppression — groups below the minimum are withheld.
  • Compiler and execution — parameterised SQL against materialised metrics.
  • Lineage attachment — every returned value carries its pin ID.

Prompts are versioned files in prompts/*.yaml with committed fixture responses, so the test suite never needs a live LLM call. If the provider is down, NLQ degrades to an honest "temporarily unavailable" banner rather than a wrong answer.

The job: a company uploads its own draft or filed BRSR and sees where it stands, privately.

Analyse is the bridge between the public corpus and a company's own position. The user uploads their report; the same parsing, extraction and scoring pipeline runs against it; and they get their completeness, substance and readiness profile plotted against the peers already in the corpus.

The privacy promise is stated before a file is selected, not in a footer after: private by default, not added to the public corpus, retention stated, deletable on request. That promise is a legal-gate item, not a marketing line.

Where this sits in the roadmap

The public promise and journey shell are live. The upload pipeline itself is the S20 session's scope. A PM should treat the Analyse tab today as a validated intent surface with the processing pipeline behind it, not as a shipped self-serve upload.

The job: turn a pile of internal documents into a filed-ready BRSR draft, without the AI quietly deciding what the company says.

Filing Studio is an authenticated workspace built around the prescribed BRSR form schema. The flow:

  • Create a filing for the organisation and financial year. The questionnaire renders section by section from the versioned form schema.
  • Upload source documents — policies, HR data, energy and water records, last year's filing.
  • Run AI mapping per section. The mapper proposes an answer for each field with a confidence and a citation into the uploaded document.
  • Review every proposal. A human accepts, edits or rejects. Bulk-accept exists but is an explicit action, and prior-year candidates are offered separately so carry-forward is a decision rather than a default.
  • Validate. The engine runs the form's completeness and consistency checks and produces a document-gap report showing what evidence is still missing.
  • Export. SEBI-format XBRL, a board-ready PDF/DOCX draft, and an assurance-readiness gap report.

The job: make the vocabulary learnable in context, for the sponsor and the newly-appointed sustainability manager alike.

Learn is a contextual aid and a hub, not a separate product. Every guided question carries its own explanation inline; the Library holds the longer-form pieces — what BRSR Core is, what limited versus reasonable assurance means, what a substantive disclosure looks like next to boilerplate.

The Methodology page is the public, permanent version of this: it is generated from scoring.yaml, so the published method description cannot drift from the code that computes the scores.

Under the hood — the two rules that govern the whole pipeline

Fail closed. Source adapters ship disabled. SOURCE_NSE_BRSR_ENABLED defaults to false, and flipping it in production configuration is the technical expression of the legal gate signature. Requests are rate-limited globally at ACQUISITION_RATE_PER_SECOND (default 0.5 — one request every two seconds).

Withhold rather than guess. An unresolvable unit or scale withholds that single value; it does not abort the batch and no factor is invented. Three FY25 issuers file energy totals under a bare J token at mutually inconsistent magnitudes — TVSMOTOR at 676,660 and JINDALSTEL at 239.51 cannot be the same unit — so the token establishes no scale and those nine values stay unpublished. A wrongly scaled figure silently corrupts every intensity derived from it, which is a worse outcome than a gap.

How it works

The rules that decide whether a number may be published

This platform publishes figures about named companies. Five mechanisms stand between an extracted value and a public screen.

1 · Pinned field versions

A metric or score cannot exist without a field_version_pins row, and a database trigger validates that the pin, the extracted field and the filing all agree. Re-extraction produces a new version; the pin stays until a human moves it.

2 · Versioned scoring methods

Every score stores its method version, its component values, a configuration hash and its pinned anchor. The current corpus carries 1.1.0-provisional-nse. Method changes are visible, comparable and never silent — the methodology page regenerates from scoring.yaml, and a score-drift dashboard exists specifically to review the effect of a version bump before it ships.

3 · Cohort suppression

Sector percentiles are suppressed below 5 companies. Twelve of seventeen FY25 sector cohorts are below that minimum, some at n=1 where the "sector average" is one company's own value. Those cohorts are shown with an explicit footnote rather than blanked, but their percentiles stay withheld.

4 · Withhold rather than guess

Sixteen FY25 values are currently withheld: seven unresolved turnover scales and nine energy facts under an ambiguous unit token. Turnover scale is resolved from a reviewed per-issuer registry with evidence and confidence, never inferred from magnitude — a rule that exists because magnitude inference had put Coal India's turnover at ₹14,337 crore instead of ₹1,43,369 crore and its energy intensity ten times too high.

5 · Three human gates

Unsigned

Legal gate

Counsel reviews source acquisition terms, the upload privacy and retention promise, the methodology and correction pages, and the Studio liability line. Enabling a source adapter in production configuration is the signature.

Unsigned

Editorial gate

No company-level figure goes public below the confidence threshold or without QA-sampled extraction accuracy at target. Two decisions are open: whether provisional NSE pins may appear on public surfaces, and whether the cohort minimum of 5 is right.

Unsigned

UX gate

Comprehension, accessibility and expert-bypass checks must pass on the deployed release candidate. Currently blocked: the visual and keyboard passes need a browser runtime that the build environment does not yet provide.

Launch blocker, stated plainly

All three gate files exist under docs/gates/ and all three are unsigned. The build plan is explicit that there is no launch without them. A new PM's first substantive act should be to get these three signatures scheduled with the right humans — this is a coordination task, not an engineering one, and it is on the critical path.

How it works

Stack and services

Deliberately light. One database, one queue, one node — chosen so a small team can operate it.

Frontend
React 18 + TypeScript + Vite, Tailwind, ECharts via a wrapped chart kit. Public routes prerendered for indexability.
API
FastAPI on Python 3.12/3.13, SQLAlchemy async, Alembic migrations (head 0011), Pydantic schemas per router.
Workers
Celery with Redis broker plus a Beat scheduler. Task families: acquire, parse, extract, score, studio, exportgen, engagement.
Data
PostgreSQL 16 with pgvector — relational spine, raw facts, embeddings and events in one database. No Elastic, no warehouse.
Storage
S3-compatible object store for immutable raw filings and generated artifacts.
LLM
Provider-abstracted with versioned prompts in prompts/*.yaml and committed fixtures. Production configuration rejects LLM_PROVIDER=fake.
Config as data
scoring.yaml, plans.yaml, events.yaml, leads.yaml, taxonomy/*.yaml — the analytics, pricing, scoring and mapping behaviour lives in reviewable files.
Under the hood — the analytics and lead engine

Product analytics are first-party and schema-governed. events.yaml declares every permitted event and its payload; api/app/services/track.py validates against it. Events land in a monthly-partitioned events table (see docs/adr/0001-events-partitioning.md) and roll up nightly into event_daily_aggregates.

leads.yaml defines the scoring model: which behaviours signal intent, how they weight, and where a qualified lead routes. High-intent actions — repeated peer-gap views, gap-report generation, deep-dive requests — produce a scored lead with context for the BD team, visible at /admin/leads with an outcome field so the model can be tuned against reality.

Privacy is enforced in the same layer: event payloads may never contain report text, uploaded content, natural-language questions, semantic query text, company names, emails or organisation names. Users can export and delete their data from /privacy.

Running it

Operator commands

Verbs live in the Makefile. These are the ones a maintainer runs.

Bring the stack up locally

# Python 3.12–3.13, Node 20, pnpm 9, uv, Docker Compose
cp .env.example .env
make bootstrap    # uv sync + pnpm install
make up           # compose up --build -d
make migrate      # alembic upgrade head
make seed         # access fixtures + taxonomy only — no company data
make verify       # schema lint · ruff · mypy · pytest · tsc · eslint · vitest

make seed creates access fixtures and taxonomy definitions only. It deliberately creates no fictional company or filing data — that was removed in favour of the real importer, and the distinction matters: nothing in this product should ever show a made-up company.

Build and extend the corpus

# first cohort — the first 25 rows of NSE's official NIFTY 50 CSV order
make ingest-nse-initial NSE_FY=2025

# next batch — advances the persisted registry cursor
make ingest-nse-next NSE_FY=2025 NSE_LIMIT=10

# revisit registered companies without advancing the cursor;
# replaces an artifact only when the official XBRL checksum changed
make ingest-nse-refresh NSE_FY=2025 NSE_LIMIT=10

# re-map and re-pin after a mapping review, without re-fetching
make publish-nse NSE_FY=2025

# deterministic full rebuild of metrics and scores
make rebuild-metrics

Each command prints its own audit line — discovered / fetched / parsed / missing / errors, then published / pinned / mapping_missing / withheld / metrics / scores. Every run is also written to ingestion_runs. A failed company never aborts the rest of the batch.

Scheduled refresh

NSE_BRSR_SCHEDULE_ENABLED=true
NSE_BRSR_REFRESH_HOURS=168        # weekly
NSE_BRSR_DEFAULT_FY=2025
NSE_BRSR_DEFAULT_BATCH_SIZE=10

Restart scheduler and worker after changing these. New financial-year filings arrive seasonally, so the annual reprocess is a planned batch event with a budget, not a surprise.

The refresh checklist, before any cohort run

Confirm the source gate and the monitored contact in NSE_BRSR_CONTACT are current → set NSE_BRSR_DEFAULT_FY to the portal's fyTo → run a one-company canary with initial --start 0 --limit 1 → check /admin/ingestion for a parsed filing with non-zero facts → run the real cohort → review errors and source revisions before publishing.

Under the hood — two traps that have already bitten this repo
  • Single-file bind mounts pin an inode. scoring.yaml, plans.yaml and events.yaml are mounted individually. An editor that replaces the file rather than writing in place leaves the container serving the old content. Restart api, worker and scheduler after editing them.
  • The documented publish commands run inside the api container, so that container mounts current worker/ code rather than the copy baked into the image. Dev and prod compose files must stay in parity here — a mismatch previously left api and worker serving stale configuration.

Running it

Known limits — read before promising anything

A new PM will be asked what this product can claim. These are the honest boundaries as of the FY25 NIFTY 100 corpus.

  • Every published environmental figure is provisional. The mappings are declared, reviewable and evidence-linked, but none has domain sign-off yet. The product labels this on every surface; a PM must not drop the label in a deck.
  • The substance index is not yet meaningful on this corpus. It scores 20.00 for all 92 companies, because substance measures specificity in narrative text and the XBRL path carries no narrative. It becomes real when PDF text extraction runs against the same filings — the PDFs are already downloaded.
  • Assurance flags cover 25 filings, not 100. Treat cohort-wide assurance claims as unsupported today.
  • Cohorts are thin. Twelve of seventeen sectors are under the suppression minimum. Cross-sector comparison is a screening tool, not a verdict.
  • Reported figures are not a performance ranking. NTPC reports 327 million tCO2e Scope 1+2 because it generates a large share of India's electricity. Boundaries, scale and estimation method must be read before any comparison — which is why every ranking view offers a normalise-by-turnover follow-up.
  • Some filings say implausible things. Hindustan Unilever's filing reports Scope 2 emissions of zero. The portal shows what was filed and flags it for review; it does not correct an issuer's disclosure silently.
  • Market-cap band is uniform. All 100 companies are large, so the "quality by market-cap band" question has nothing to compare until the corpus extends beyond NIFTY 100.
  • Batch 1 facts predate the decimals column and are null until re-ingested. No published number depends on it — and note that XBRL decimals is precision, not scale, and must never be used to infer scale.

What it can claim, without qualification

Complete, checksummed, losslessly-persisted coverage of the NIFTY 100's FY25 BRSR filings, with 204,880 raw facts queryable, common-unit conversions for energy, water and emissions, turnover-normalised intensities, and full source lineage on every published figure. No competitor product in this market attaches evidence to the number.

Reference

Glossary

TermWhat it means here
BRSRBusiness Responsibility and Sustainability Report — SEBI's mandated annual sustainability disclosure for large listed companies.
BRSR CoreThe subset of BRSR attributes requiring reasonable assurance. Carries double weight in the completeness score.
XBRLThe machine-readable format the filing is tagged in. Each tagged value is a "fact" with a concept, unit, context and period.
DCYMainThe XBRL context for the issuer's entity-wide current financial year. Facts are read from this context so facility-level breakdowns do not double-count. DPYMain is the prior year.
CompletenessWeighted field coverage on the QA-pinned disclosure set. Says information is present — not that it is specific or assured.
SubstanceAn index combining quantified targets (35%), dated commitments (25%), named methodologies (20%) and corpus originality (20%). Screens for specificity, not correctness.
BoilerplateA near-verbatim 8-word phrase appearing in at least 3 companies' filings. The shared-phrase table regenerates from the pinned corpus on every rebuild.
Assurance readinessAssurance status (40%) + BRSR Core coverage (35%) + complete source lineage (25%). Not a statement that anything has been independently assured.
PinA field_version_pins row locking one immutable extracted-field version as the published one. Metrics and scores reference pins, not raw fields.
WithheldA value that exists in the filing but cannot be resolved to a trustworthy unit or scale. Published as a gap with a reason, never as a guess.
SuppressedA cohort statistic hidden because the group has fewer than 5 companies and would identify or misrepresent individuals.
ProvisionalA mapping or figure that is declared and evidence-linked but has not been signed off by a domain reviewer.
